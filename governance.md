# Governance

## What this platform is

Thousand and One Stories is a platform for fiction, structured around communities — each one curated by an editor with genuine taste. Writers submit. Readers discover. No algorithms decide what surfaces.

The model is closer to a literary magazine than a social network. Quality is determined by human judgment, not engagement metrics.

---

## Communities

Anyone can propose a community. The person who proposes it becomes its founding editor.

A community has a name, a description, submission guidelines, and an editor. It exists to publish a specific kind of writing — defined by genre, form, voice, region, or whatever the editor decides is the animating principle.

**Before a community appears on the front page, it must have:**
- An editor with a public bio
- Submission guidelines
- At least 3 published, approved stories

Communities that don't meet this bar exist but aren't listed publicly. This prevents the dead-subreddit problem.

---

## Editors

Editors are the heart of the platform. They:

- Set the submission guidelines for their community
- Approve or reject submitted stories
- Choose which approved story is featured (pinned to the top)
- Represent the community's aesthetic and voice

**How editors are chosen:**

Initially, the platform founder serves as editor of all communities — seeding each one with real content and establishing the quality bar before opening them to community governance.

Once a community has an established readership, editors are elected by the community. *(Election mechanics are an open question — see below.)*

Anyone can found a new community and serve as its editor from the start.

---

## Platform authority

Some decisions belong to the platform, not to individual communities:

- Removing content that is illegal or violates basic terms of service
- Removing or suspending communities that become inactive or violate platform rules
- Defining the minimum requirements for front-page listing

Everything else — taste, voice, what gets published — belongs to the community and its editor.

---

## Open questions

**Election mechanics**
- Who is eligible to vote in an editor election — readers, writers, or both?
- What triggers an election — a petition threshold, a scheduled term, or editor resignation?
- What is the quorum or vote threshold required?
- Are there term limits?

**Editor removal and succession**
- What happens if a founding editor abandons a community — does it go to election, get archived, or transfer to an active member?
- Is there a recall process if the community loses confidence in an editor?
- How long does inactivity need to be before the platform intervenes?

**Writer incentives**
- Beyond audience, what do writers get for publishing here — pay, prestige, something else?
- Does the platform take any revenue share if monetization is introduced later?

**Platform removal of communities**
- Under what conditions can the platform remove a community entirely?
- Is there an appeals process?

**Moderation within communities**
- Can editors ban users from submitting to their community?
- What recourse does a writer have if they believe a rejection was in bad faith?

## Editor fees

Take a cut when a writer pays an editor for work. Why wouldn't they just go outside the platform? How does Upwork do it?

## Submission fees

Writers pay to submit to a community and the platform takes a cut. Issues:

* This makes the writer the customer, when I want the reader to be.


# Claude ideas:

 On the Upwork question: they prevent leakage primarily through trust infrastructure — escrow, dispute resolution, reviews tied to the platform profile. If a writer pays an editor outside, they lose those
  guarantees. The bet is that the platform's reputation system is worth the fee.

  For this platform, the same logic could apply: editors build their reputation on the platform (story counts, community quality, reader engagement). An editor who takes work off-platform loses their
  standing here. That's the lock-in.

  A few other angles worth noting:

  - Reader-as-customer via tipping/patronage — readers tip writers or communities they love. Platform takes a cut. Keeps reader as the valued party.
  - Editor subscriptions — editors pay a small monthly fee for the tools/audience, not writers or readers. Similar to how Substack charges nothing but takes 10% of subscriptions.
  - Sponsored communities — a lit magazine, MFA program, or publisher sponsors a community as a discovery/pipeline tool.
